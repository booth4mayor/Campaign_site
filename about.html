Questions people ask

Straight answers

Replace these with the questions you actually get asked at doors. Answering the hard ones in public is worth more than avoiding them.

What does the Mayor of Elmira actually do?

[This is worth answering carefully and honestly. Elmira operates under a council-manager form of government, which means day-to-day administration runs through a City Manager. Explain what that means for what a mayor can and can't promise — and then explain where the office does carry real weight: setting the agenda, appointments, the budget vote, and the public voice of the city.]

[A candidate who explains the limits of the office honestly is more credible on everything else he says.]

Have you held office before?

[Answer directly. If this is your first run, say so and then say what you've done instead — the meetings you've attended, the records you've read, the work you've already put in. First-time candidacy is only a weakness if you're evasive about it.]

Will you raise taxes?

[Do not dodge this one. Say what you believe, and say what would have to be true for your answer to change. Voters have heard absolute pledges before and discount them; a candidate who explains his reasoning is more believable than one who makes a promise no mayor can unilaterally keep.]
